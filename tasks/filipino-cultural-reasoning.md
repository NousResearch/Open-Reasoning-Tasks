# Filipino Cultural Reasoning

## Description

This task evaluates the model's ability to understand and reason about Filipino cultural contexts, traditions, values, and social norms. The model must demonstrate cultural sensitivity and contextual awareness when analyzing scenarios specific to Philippine society.

## Purpose

- Test cultural intelligence and context-awareness
- Evaluate ability to navigate nuanced social situations
- Assess understanding of Filipino values (e.g., pakikisama, hiya, utang na loob)
- Measure capability to provide culturally appropriate advice or analysis

## Examples

### Example 1: Understanding "Mañana Habit"

**Input:**
A foreign investor is frustrated because their Filipino business partners keep saying "bukas na lang" (maybe tomorrow) whenever urgent decisions need to be made. The investor interprets this as laziness and unprofessionalism. Analyze this cultural misunderstanding and provide insights.

**Expected Reasoning:**
The model should:
1. Recognize "mañana habit" as a cultural tendency, not laziness
2. Explain Filipino conflict avoidance and indirect communication
3. Consider face-saving behavior (hiya)
4. Suggest culturally appropriate communication strategies
5. Balance respect for Filipino culture with business efficiency needs

**Sample Output:**
The "mañana habit" reflects Filipino cultural values rather than unprofessionalism. Filipinos often avoid direct confrontation and use indirect communication to maintain harmony (pakikisama) and avoid embarrassment (hiya). "Bukas na lang" may indicate:
- Need for more consensus-building
- Discomfort with the proposal but reluctance to say no directly
- Need for more time to consult with the group

Recommended approach:
- Build personal relationships first (pakikipagkapwa-tao)
- Allow time for group consultation
- Frame requests more gently
- Create private spaces for honest feedback
- Understand that relationships take priority over rigid schedules

### Example 2: "Balikbayan Box" Expectations

**Input:**
A Filipino nurse working abroad is stressed because relatives expect expensive gifts every time she visits. She wants to refuse but fears being labeled as "mayabang" (arrogant) or "nakalimot sa pinagmulan" (forgot her roots). What factors should she consider?

**Expected Reasoning:**
The model should:
1. Recognize the concept of "utang na loob" (debt of gratitude)
2. Understand family expectations in Filipino culture
3. Consider the pressure of being an OFW (Overseas Filipino Worker)
4. Balance personal boundaries with cultural obligations
5. Provide nuanced advice that respects both autonomy and tradition

**Sample Output:**
This scenario involves complex Filipino cultural values:

Key considerations:
- **Utang na loob**: Deep sense of reciprocal obligation to family
- **Balikbayan expectations**: Cultural norm where OFWs are expected to share success
- **Family collectivism**: Individual success seen as family achievement
- **Social pressure**: Fear of gossip and family judgment

Potential approaches:
1. **Set boundaries gradually**: Don't cut off support abruptly
2. **Communicate financial reality**: Share struggles openly with close family
3. **Redirect expectations**: Suggest practical gifts or shared experiences
4. **Invest in family education**: Help them understand your financial situation
5. **Find middle ground**: Maintain relationships while protecting your finances

The nurse should recognize that saying "no" doesn't mean forgetting her roots, but establishing healthy boundaries is important for long-term sustainability.

### Example 3: Workplace "Ninong/Ninang" Culture

**Input:**
A new Filipino employee is uncomfortable being asked to be "ninong" (godparent) at a colleague's child's baptism after only working together for 2 months. They barely know each other outside work. Should they accept? What does this request really mean?

**Expected Reasoning:**
The model should:
1. Understand the significance of compadrazgo system
2. Recognize this as relationship-building, not just a favor
3. Consider workplace dynamics and pakikisama
4. Evaluate long-term implications
5. Provide culturally aware guidance

**Sample Output:**
Being asked to be ninong/ninang is culturally significant:

What it means:
- **Not just honorary**: Creates lifelong bond (compadre/comadre)
- **Signal of trust**: Colleague sees you as worthy of this role
- **Workplace integration**: Invitation to be part of inner circle
- **Future expectations**: May involve financial support for inaanak (godchild)

Considerations:
- Refusing may hurt workplace relationships (pakikisama)
- Accepting creates obligations (school tuition help, gifts at special occasions)
- This is how Filipinos expand their support network

Recommendations:
- If you plan long-term employment: Consider accepting to build deeper relationships
- If you're uncomfortable: Politely decline citing personal circumstances or existing commitments
- If accepting: Understand you're joining an extended family network
- Be clear about boundaries from the start

The request is a compliment and shows inclusion, but also comes with real responsibilities.

## Evaluation Criteria

A strong response should:
1. **Demonstrate cultural knowledge**: Show understanding of Filipino values
2. **Avoid stereotyping**: Recognize diversity within Filipino culture
3. **Provide nuanced analysis**: Not reduce culture to simple rules
4. **Balance perspectives**: Consider multiple viewpoints
5. **Offer practical guidance**: Give actionable, culturally sensitive advice
6. **Show respect**: Avoid judgmental or condescending tone
